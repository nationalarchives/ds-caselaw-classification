# Candidate public-facing top-level taxonomy (v2, legislation-aligned)

## Purpose and design choice

This is the second version of the [candidate public-facing top-level taxonomy](candidate-public-top-level-taxonomy.md), rebuilt against the updated draft legislation thesaurus ([thesaurus-current](../taxonomy-extractions/thesaurus-current.md)) using the findings of the [legislation thesaurus comparison](legislation-thesaurus-comparison.md).

Design decisions carried over from the first draft and the comparison:

- **Problem-led wording is retained.** Headings still describe life situations and interactions (a home, a job, a dispute with the state), not law-school branches. Where the legislation wording is already clear and public-facing, it is kept as the primary label and lay aliases can sit behind it.
- **Legislation Subject areas are the canonical layer.** Each public heading names the legislation Subject areas it covers (see the comparison's recommendation to make the legislation scheme canonical). A heading that spans several Subject areas is a **non-exclusive public grouping**, not a new canonical parent.
- **Where a Subject area does not plausibly generate case law, it is omitted** from the case-law top level — but the omission is recorded here rather than silently dropped.

Choices made with the investigation sponsor:

- *Health and Public Health* and *Social Care and Wellbeing* are merged into one heading; a case about care services rarely distinguishes them.
- *Environment and Nature* and *Energy and Net Zero* are **separate** headings: energy markets and climate duties generate distinctive case law.
- *Agriculture and Rural Affairs*, *Animals, Pets and Wildlife* and the countryside parts of the environment area are merged; *Food* stays separate because food standards and hygiene cases are a distinct regulatory domain.
- *Public and Personal Safety* keeps its **own** heading rather than being dissolved into crime.
- Omitted entirely as not arising in case law: **Analysis and Information**, **Economy**, **Science, Research and Innovation**. Retained for now: **Religion**, **International Affairs and Diplomacy**, **Regulation of Government Spend and Services**.
- The doctrinal *Civil Law* area is presented in a single problem-led heading alongside *Rights of the Individual* rather than as its own top level.

The result is **28 headings** — wider than v1's 17, which is the deliberate consequence of preserving the thesaurus's distinctions (see [legislation-thesaurus-comparison](legislation-thesaurus-comparison.md), "Split the most consequential mergers").

## Deliberately omitted Subject areas

The following legislation Subject areas are omitted from the case-law top level because they are about the classification and management of legislation and government information rather than disputes courts decide. If cases touching them appear in practice, attach them to the nearest heading and keep the Subject tag.

- **Analysis and Information** (official statistics, public records, economic policy) — public records and statistics occasionally feature in judicial review; those cases sit under *Government, Parliament and the constitution*.
- **Economy** (budget, currency, national debt) — macroeconomic policy rarely appears as such; fiscal measures surface through *Tax* and *Government finance and procurement*.
- **Science, Research and Innovation** (research funding, AI strategy, measurement standards) — disputes about research funding are public-law cases under *Government, Parliament and the constitution* or *Business, trade and industry*.

Note also that **Civil Law** is not given its own heading: it is doctrinal (contract, tort, trusts, property, family) rather than problem-led, and its content is distributed across the topic headings and gathered under *Your rights and the state*. The **Business** (NACE sectors) and **Regions** partitions are separate dimensions and are not top-level headings here (see the cross-cutting facets below).

## Cross-cutting facets (unchanged in principle)

As in v1, keep these as separate dimensions rather than subject headings:

- **Procedure and stage:** first instance, appeal, judicial review, interim relief, enforcement, tribunal/court procedure.
- **Dispute method:** litigation, arbitration, mediation, settlement.
- **Legal role/institution:** court, tribunal, police, regulator, local authority, professional, lawyer.
- **People and circumstances:** children, disability, protected characteristics, victim, defendant, tenant, worker, business size.
- **Geography and jurisdiction:** England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, UK-wide, international/cross-border — aligned with the thesaurus **Regions** partition and extent/application metadata, which remain separate.
- **Sector:** the thesaurus **Business** partition (NACE) tags affected sectors and is not a subject.
- **International law and conflicts:** attach to the underlying subject, with an *International / cross-border* tag; cases about public international law itself also go under *International affairs and treaties* and/or *Government, Parliament and the constitution*.
- **Rare institutional subjects:** ecclesiastical, military and other specialist matters now have explicit headings (*Religion*, *National security, defence and emergencies*); assign precisely rather than inventing an "other law" catch-all.

## Why this version differs from v1

1. **Faithfulness to the canonical scheme beats a small front door.** The comparison concluded that 17 problem-led headings were too compressed to share with the legislation thesaurus; the v2 list preserves distinctions the comparison flagged as consequential (tax vs banking/finance; criminal law vs justice institutions; environment vs energy vs agriculture vs animals; government vs local government vs procurement).
2. **Problem-led wording is still primary.** The heading wording describes situations; the legislation Subject labels remain the canonical concepts behind each heading and can carry their own public display labels.
3. **Multi-assignment is explicit.** Because concepts are duplicated across Subject areas (animal welfare, consumer rights, data protection, planning, workplace safety), a case may be tagged to several Subject areas and several public headings.
4. **Omissions are documented.** Three Subject areas are dropped with the routing explained, rather than hidden inside mergers.

## Validation plan

- Crosswalk the headings against the top-level terms of the gathered case-law schemes; the first pass (Sargeant et al., ICLR, Lands Chamber) is in [Mapping v2 headings to example case-law schemes](v2-heading-scheme-mapping.md).
- Run the v2 headings against a stratified sample of judgments from every court/tribunal and subject area, especially tax, planning/property, public law, commercial, family, criminal, immigration and Scotland/NI cases.
- Record "uncertain" fits and reviewer overrides; specifically test whether *Your rights and the state* is understood as a substantive heading and whether *Public and personal safety* draws cases distinct from *Crime*.
- Test the wording with non-lawyers: can they select a heading from a real-life question and predict what is inside it?
