# Comparing the candidate case-law top level with the draft legislation thesaurus

## Overall assessment

There is **strong thematic overlap but only moderate structural alignment**. Both schemes cover the same broad social and policy domains. The proposed 17-heading case-law list, however, deliberately combines domains into everyday problem areas; the draft thesaurus has about 37 Subject top-level areas, reflecting policy responsibilities and legislative coverage. The case-law list is easier to browse as a small front door, but it is too compressed to become the canonical shared vocabulary without losing distinctions important to legislation.

For a unified scheme **biased toward the legislation thesaurus**, use its Subject partition and identifiers as the canonical top-level structure. Adapt its display labels and definitions for public comprehension, but do not collapse its areas merely to preserve the 17-heading count. The previous proposal is best used as a set of **lay aliases / optional browse groupings**, and as a source of synonyms and user language.

The thesaurus's Business partition (NACE sectors) and Regions partition have different meanings from Subject. Keep them as separate dimensions, not as Subject branches. Case-law metadata such as court, jurisdiction, procedural stage and remedy should likewise remain separate facets.

## What the 17-heading proposal misses or under-represents

The candidate can point to a nearby heading for most of these, but it does not preserve their status as distinct subject areas. A legislation-led common scheme should retain them as headings or clearly identifiable concepts:

- **Food** and food standards/safety.
- **Energy and Net Zero**, distinct from general environment and natural resources.
- **National Security, Defence and Emergencies**.
- **Religion** and ecclesiastical matters.
- **Science, Research and Innovation**.
- **Analysis and Information** (statistics, public records, economic analysis).
- **Economy** and public finance, distinct from personal money and tax.
- **Local Government**, **Public Procurement**, and **Other Government and Community Organisations**.
- **Public and Personal Safety**, separate from criminal offences and policing.
- **Culture, Media and Sport**, including heritage, gambling and tourism.
- **International Affairs and Diplomacy**, beyond immigration and cross-border private disputes.
- **Animals, Pets and Wildlife**, distinct from farming and environmental protection.

These are not edge cases from the legislation perspective: they are explicit areas in the draft and may be central to identifying the policy subject of an Act or instrument.

## Changes I recommend

### 1. Make the legislation Subject scheme canonical

Keep its current Subject concepts and hierarchy as the authoritative shared layer. Legislation and case law should point to the same concept identifiers wherever they genuinely concern the same subject. Do not create parallel “case-law versions” of concepts such as *Housing and Planning*, *Employment, Jobs and Workplace* or *Taxation*.

### 2. Use the case-law proposal as a public access layer, not a replacement hierarchy

Retain its plain-language phrases as preferred public labels, aliases, search terms or browse collections. Where a public heading spans several thesaurus areas, make it a **non-exclusive grouping** rather than a new canonical parent. For example, “Money, debt, tax and financial services” can group Banking and Finance, Economy and Taxation in a public browse view while the assigned subject tags remain distinct.

### 3. Split the most consequential mergers

If the 17 headings are retained as an actual top-level taxonomy rather than a browse layer, at minimum split:

- **Money, debt, tax and financial services** into *Banking and Finance*, *Economy*, and *Taxation* (with plain-language display names).
- **Crime, safety and policing** into *Criminal Law*, *Justice and Law Enforcement*, *Public and Personal Safety*, and *National Security, Defence and Emergencies*.
- **Environment, animals and countryside** into *Environment and Nature*, *Energy and Net Zero*, *Agriculture and Rural Affairs*, and *Animals, Pets and Wildlife*.
- **Government decisions, public services and rights** into *Constitution, Parliament and Government*, *Local Government*, *Rights of the Individual*, *Public Procurement*, and *Other Government and Community Organisations*.
- **Digital life, information, media and creativity** into *Technology, Data and Communication*, *Culture, Media and Sport*, and the relevant rights/IP concepts.
- **Business, trade and professional activity** from consumer protection, public procurement and sector tagging.

Given these splits and the omitted areas above, matching the legislation scheme's roughly 37 Subject headings is less disruptive and more faithful than maintaining 17 canonical headings.

### 4. Improve public wording with labels and definitions, not broad mergers

Some draft headings already work well for the public (e.g. *Housing and Planning*, *Education, Skills and Children*, *Employment, Jobs and Workplace*, *Immigration, Visas and Citizenship*). Where a term is formal or ambiguous, add a public display label, definition and synonyms rather than changing its identity. Potential label refinements include:

- *Banking and Finance* → “Banking, money and financial services”
- *Criminal Law* → “Crime and criminal offences”
- *Justice and Law Enforcement* → “Courts, police and legal services” (or keep the heading and define its scope carefully)
- *Constitution, Parliament and Government (State)* → “Government, Parliament and the constitution”
- *Life Events* → “Birth, family changes, later life and death”
- *Boundaries, Borders and Land Management* → “Land, boundaries and border controls” (with a scope note because customs and outer space currently sit beneath it)

These are examples for testing, not final editorial decisions. Some existing labels bundle concepts that may need a hierarchy review as well as a wording change.

_Those basic renamings have been carried out and the [Thesaurus-current](../taxonomy-extractions/thesaurus-current.md) page has been updated to match._

### 5. Keep life events and public language available across domains

The draft's **Life Events** area is valuable and more complete than the case-law proposal's late-life-only heading. Preserve it, but recognise that events such as marriage, birth, retirement, bereavement and moving home also map to substantive subjects. Use cross-links or a “browse by life event” collection rather than forcing every event into an exclusive branch.

### 6. Preserve polyhierarchy and document duplicate concepts

The draft intentionally repeats concepts across areas (for example animal welfare, consumer rights, data protection, public records, planning, and safety topics). Model each as one concept with multiple broader links, or as a single canonical term with references, rather than creating unrelated duplicates. Record preferred term, alternate labels, definition, scope note, broader/narrower relations and stable identifier.

## Recommended unified architecture

1. **Shared Subject thesaurus:** the legislation Subject partition, with reviewed public-facing labels, stable IDs, definitions and cross-references. Used for both legislation and judgments; allow multi-assignment.
2. **Public browse groupings:** optional lay-oriented collections derived from the 17-heading proposal. These can be many-to-many and can evolve without changing the canonical Subject concepts.
3. **Business sector:** retain the NACE-based Business partition as an independent classification of affected sectors/organisations, not as a legal subject.
4. **Geographical reference:** retain Regions as an independent search refinement, separate from legal extent/application and from the case's court/jurisdiction metadata.
5. **Case-law facets:** court/tribunal, procedural posture, decision type, remedy, parties/roles and jurisdiction remain separate metadata dimensions.

This keeps the legislation thesaurus's policy coverage and granularity while importing the case-law proposal's main strength: a public should be able to enter through familiar words and situations, then be routed to precise, consistent subject concepts.
