 # Candidate public-facing top-level taxonomy

## Purpose and design choice

This is a proposed **browse vocabulary for the top level**, not a replacement for legal subject indexing. It combines the life-problem language of Citizens Advice, LAMS CCS and Stanford LIST with the subject coverage needed for UK judgments in Sargeant et al. and ICLR. The aim is that a non-lawyer can choose a recognisable starting point, while every label has a maintainable route to doctrinal subjects.

The candidate has 17 main headings. That is deliberately close to the workable 14-heading LAMS CCS and the 20-domain Stanford LIST, but broadens the advice-sector schemes to cover criminal justice, commerce, courts, public law and less common specialist subjects found in case law.

## Important cross-cutting subjects

Some dimensions should not be forced into one top-level life-area. Keep them as additional labels or facets, with optional links to one or more public headings:

- **Procedure and stage:** first instance, appeal, judicial review, interim relief, enforcement, tribunal/court procedure.
- **Dispute method:** litigation, arbitration, mediation, settlement.
- **Legal role/institution:** court, tribunal, police, regulator, local authority, professional, lawyer.
- **People and circumstances:** children, disability, protected characteristics, victim, defendant, tenant, worker, business size.
- **Geography and jurisdiction:** England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, UK-wide, international/cross-border.
- **International law and conflicts:** normally attach to the underlying subject; use an additional *International / cross-border* tag. Where a case is about public international law itself (e.g. state immunity or treaty obligations), place it under Government decisions, public services and rights as well.
- **Rare institutional subjects:** ecclesiastical, military, charities, and specialist natural-resource disputes can be assigned to the closest public heading and retain their precise doctrinal terms. Do not create a broad “other law” heading merely to house rare catchwords.

## Why this top level is a reasonable starting point

1. **Recognisable, problem-led wording.** Most labels resemble everyday situations or public-service interactions rather than law-school branches. That borrows from the convergence in CA, LAMS and LIST.
2. **Broader than advice-site coverage.** Criminal justice, business, court process, tax, public law, IP/media, environment and transport are retained because case-law corpora include them even when advice-site navigation does not.
3. **Compatible with doctrine, not a substitute for it.** Each public heading can connect to several precise doctrine terms. For example, “Home, housing and land” can map to land law, landlord and tenant, mortgage, conveyancing, planning and nuisance without asking the public to choose among those doctrines first.
4. **Not exclusive.** A case about a worker's discrimination claim may be tagged both *Work* and *Government… / rights*; an eviction-benefits dispute may be *Home* and *Benefits*. Multi-label assignment is preferable to an artificial single “main area” decision.
5. **Keep non-subject facets separate.** ICLR's single Legal Topic is useful for a simple browse filter, but its catchwords show why a case also needs several subjects. Court, procedure, remedy and jurisdiction should not be disguised as subject headings.

## Suggested mapping model and validation

- Give each public heading a stable identifier, a short definition, synonyms/search terms, and explicit include/exclude notes.
- Link public headings to controlled doctrinal concepts using a **many-to-many crosswalk**; preserve the source scheme and source term for provenance. Do not infer that a crosswalk is a legal equivalence.
- Permit multiple public headings per case, with one optional primary heading only for display. Keep doctrine/catchwords as a separate, multi-valued layer.
- Validate coverage against a stratified sample of judgments from every court/tribunal and subject area, especially criminal, tax, planning/property, public law, commercial, family and Scotland/NI. Record “uncertain” and reviewer overrides rather than silently forcing a fit.
- Test the wording with people who are not lawyers: can they select a heading from a real-life question, and can they predict what is inside it? Use search logs and query-to-heading success as measures if available.
- Treat this as a candidate for user testing, not a settled production list. In particular, test whether *Money, debt, tax and financial services* is too wide, whether *Courts, disputes and legal help* is understood as a topic rather than a navigation tool, and whether the distinction between *Benefits* and other public services is clear.
